You are mining a community post (Reddit thread or blog/forum article) for IDEAS
that could make AI-generated images pass as real photographs and thereby evade
VLM-based fake-image detectors (FakeVLM, FakeShield, SIDA).

WHAT WE WANT — the IDEA, not a finished recipe.
We are NOT asking for exact PIL/OpenCV parameters. We want the *concept* and the
*mechanism*: the intuition a practitioner described for why an AI image looks fake
and what they do about it. A downstream coding agent will turn each idea into a
concrete tool later. Example of a good extraction:
  idea: "photograph or re-scan the AI image at a slight tilt, like a phone snapshot
         of a printout"
  mechanism: "introduces perspective skew, re-photography sensor noise and JPEG
              history that real shared photos have but clean AI renders lack"
That is fully acceptable EVEN THOUGH no exact rotation angle is given.

A method may be a single step or many steps — STEP COUNT DOES NOT MATTER.
Do NOT discard an idea just because it is "only one operation". Judge ideas by
NOVELTY and FEASIBILITY (defined below), not by complexity.

CLASSIFY each extracted idea into one method_type:
  - "generative" : uses a generative model to do the work — T2I/img2img prompt
                   tricks, negative prompts, low-denoise regeneration, LoRA/
                   checkpoint swaps, diffusion inpainting of artifacts.
  - "numeric"    : a deterministic signal/pixel operation or pipeline — grain,
                   compression, sensor-noise, color/tone, geometric, capture-pipeline
                   mimicry. No neural model needed at apply time.
  - "hybrid"     : explicitly combines the two (e.g. numeric pipeline THEN a
                   generative regeneration pass, or generate-then-launder).

SKIP only genuinely empty content: venting, news/ethics commentary, pure
"is this fake?" guessing with no technique, or vague aesthetic praise with no
actionable mechanism. Set verdict="discard" and candidate_methods=[] for those.

Score the POST on three axes (1=worst, 10=best):
  - novelty     : how unusual / under-the-radar are these ideas? Favor techniques a
                  detector is UNLIKELY to have been trained against (recent tricks,
                  niche capture pipelines, creative model-side moves). Common,
                  obvious moves score low; clever or fresh ideas score high.
  - feasibility : could an LLM coding agent realistically implement these as an
                  image tool? (a PIL/OpenCV op, or a call to a local generative
                  model). Concrete, mechanism-clear ideas score high; hand-wavy or
                  research-grade ("train a new GAN") ideas score low.
  - relevance   : does the content actually concern making AI images look real /
                  evading detection (vs. unrelated photo editing)?

For each candidate idea capture:
  - name (snake_case, ~2-4 words)
  - idea (one sentence: the concept)
  - mechanism (one sentence: WHY it makes the image look real / why a detector
    might miss it)
  - method_type ("generative" | "numeric" | "hybrid")
  - quoted_evidence (exact phrase you took it from, <= 200 chars)
  - source ("post" | "comment_<author>")
